Event 3-online

EVENT REPORT: FCAJ - AGENTIC AI BUILD WEEK

I. Objectives & General Context

On the morning of July 25, 2026, I attended the “FCAJ - Agentic AI Build Week” event on the 26th Floor of the Bitexco Financial Tower. This was not the competition venue itself, but an inspiring sharing session narrating the journey of the winning teams from the largest Agentic AI Buildathon in the ASEAN region.

Embracing the spirit of “Show Up. Build. Pitch. WIN!”, the speakers and participating teams painted a realistic picture of AI stepping out of the conventional chatbot phase to become autonomous “Agents,” directly solving stringent infrastructure and enterprise business problems.

II. Technology Highlights & Solutions from 5 Prominent Teams

Through the presentations at the event, I learned about 5 incredible practical architectural models built in just 24 hours:

1. Team OneTeam (First Prize) - KFC Bot Agent Project

  • Problem: An AI assistant supporting multi-channel conversational ordering, helping to prevent “dropped orders” by allowing customers to order directly on Zalo/WhatsApp instead of having to download an app.
  • Technology: Applied Amazon Bedrock AgentCore to replace 60% of the infrastructure source code, achieving ultra-low latency (3-5 seconds) and optimizing costs to an extremely cheap level: only $0.006/order.

2. Team Signal Scout (Second Prize) - Strategic Signal Reconnaissance Platform

  • Problem: Helps corporate strategy teams gather fragmented information from competitors and chain it into visual analysis.
  • Technology: Used the TinyFish tool to bypass login walls to retrieve internal report data, then outputted it to a Dashboard along with specific evidence to support decision-making.

3. Team Plan V - SA Professional AI Native App Project

  • Problem: Automating tasks for Solution Architects.
  • Technology: Reads requirement documents (BRD/PRD) in natural language, automatically draws cloud diagrams on Draw.io, extracts cost estimates, generates Terraform infrastructure code (IaC), and deploys directly to AWS. The system integrates strict typing to control the AI from using prohibited services.

4. Team 3K (3KA) - S.H.E.P.H.E.R.D Project

  • Problem: An intelligent system for assessing, detecting risks, and coordinating human flow at airports and supermarkets.
  • Technology: Applied computer vision (YOLO, ByteTrack) combined with live video (Kinesis Video Streams). Bedrock AgentCore continuously calculates density, predicts congestion, and issues proactive warnings to staff.

5. Team Six Pillar - Adaptive Workflow Engine (AML) Project

  • Problem: An Anti-Money Laundering (AML) system for banks/exchanges, minimizing the false positive rate (up to 90-95%) of traditional processing workflows.
  • Technology: Built a Multi-agent system divided into 3 stages: KYC profiling, money flow, and sanctions. The AI synthesizes evidence and proposes actions. The system applies Enterprise Trust security standards (KMS, GuardDuty, Security Hub) and is moderated via Human-in-the-loop.

III. Enterprise-grade Operations & Security Standards

  • Cost and ROI: Team OneTeam proved their excellence by optimizing AI costs to $0.006/order. Conversely, mistakes can be costly (one team’s SageMaker costs spiked to $48 in 3 hours). An enterprise product must have transparent costs (like Signal Scout’s budget estimate) and avoid token burnout.
  • Enterprise Standard Security: Team Six Pillar provided a significant lesson in security by integrating the KMS key management system, GuardDuty, and Security Hub to detect cyberattacks, affirming that financial AI must always be accompanied by robust defenses.
  • Decision Support (Human-in-the-loop): Whether it is an AML system or crowd coordination, AI only plays the role of analyzing evidence. The final decision (account blocking, security dispatch) still requires human approval.

IV. Application Orientation & Personal Lessons

  • Scope Management: The ability to successfully demo determines up to 70% of the chance of winning. A crucial piece of advice is to thoroughly apply the MVP mindset: “Small, finished work beats big, broken ideas.”
  • Just sign up: The fear of “not being good enough” or “fear of failure” is the biggest barrier. Daring to venture out and showing up is already half the battle.
  • Networking: The core value does not lie in the prize, but in the opportunity to interact with mentors and talented developers to expand one’s professional network.

V. Experiences & Real-world Perspectives at the Event

Listening to the narrations, I could clearly feel the emotional “rollercoaster” of the teams during the 24-hour Hackathon. The most valuable lessons came from the chaos itself:

  • The Harshness and “Once-in-a-lifetime” Incidents: Contestants had to stay awake until 3-4 AM, surviving on Red Bull and fried chicken. The state of sleep deprivation led to countless ironic mistakes: forgetting to commit code, accidentally pushing environment variable files (.env) to a public GitHub repository.
  • Overcoming Barriers and Ego: Teams were often patched together from various students. The Champion, Team OneTeam, is a classic example with 5 members speaking diverse languages (Indian English, American English, Vietnamese). Despite disagreements or arguments, they learned to lower their personal egos and divide work transparently to win together.
  • Future-building Mindset: As Mr. Joseph Marazota shared, the young people participating in today’s Hackathons are not limited by the “old baggage” of 20 years ago. They hold the opportunity to bring new mindset models to shape the technological future.
